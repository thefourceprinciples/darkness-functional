# Part XI — Stewardship Qualification Standard

## 75. Stewardship vector

S_DSSP = (N, L, O, H, R, C, A, P, E, D, J, X)

N = Non-Distortion

L = Load Fidelity

O = Open Futures

H = Clean Handoff

R = Reversibility

C = Containment

A = Auditability

P = Proportionality

E = Environmental and lifecycle integrity

D = Dual-use governance

J = Justice and burden distribution

X = Clean Release

A critical failure cannot be averaged away.

## 76. Article 1 — Non-Distortion

The project preserves reality more faithfully than its preferred story.

Requirements:

- raw data preserved;
- failed runs and nulls retained;
- simulation and analog labels visible;
- observation separated from interpretation;
- active contradictions disclosed;
- uncertainty present in the same communication layer as the claim;
- public language no stronger than the technical result;
- corrections propagated.

## 77. Article 2 — Load Fidelity

The project carries its full burden rather than externalizing it.

Total load includes:

scientific + physical + material + energy + environmental + human + institutional + security + future custody

A prototype is not low-cost merely because its laboratory, specialists, cryogens, infrastructure, or waste stream are omitted from the visible budget.

## 78. Article 3 — Open Futures

The project preserves credible paths to continue, pause, reverse, replace, transfer, or terminate.

Threats include:

- infrastructure lock-in;
- single-supplier dependence;
- proprietary epistemic monopoly;
- unrecoverable data formats;
- unreplaceable materials;
- strategic dependence;
- coercive adoption;
- inherited maintenance burden;
- knowledge bottlenecks.

A system with only one path forward is not stewardship-qualified.

## 79. Article 4 — Clean Handoff

Transfer includes understanding and responsibility, not merely equipment and files.

Required handoff package:

identity manifest

architecture

apparatus diagrams

software versions

raw-data locations

calibration records

claim and contradiction ledgers

hazard register

operating envelope

shutdown procedure

maintenance schedule

incident history

stewardship restrictions

release obligations

The receiving team must demonstrate shutdown, data retrieval, calibration understanding, and hazard comprehension.

## 80. Article 5 — Reversibility

DSSP tracks:

physical reversibility

operational reversibility

data reversibility

institutional reversibility

social reversibility

environmental reversibility

A device may switch off while society remains unable to function without it. That is still a reversibility and Open Futures problem.

## 81. Article 6 — Containment

Containment covers:

space

time

energy

matter

information

authority

consequence

Laboratory walls do not by themselves establish containment.

## 82. Article 7 — Auditability

An external reviewer must be able to reconstruct:

- who acted;
- what changed;
- when;
- which evidence supported it;
- which rule authorized it;
- which objections remained;
- what failed;
- and how the decision was made.

Audit layers include data, code, model, claim, gate, safety, finance, governance, and release.

## 83. Article 8 — Proportionality

The project asks:

Is this the least burdensome credible action capable of answering the question?

Prefer a smaller discriminating test to a larger ambiguous test.

Prefer existing-data analysis when it can answer the question.

Prefer safe analog qualification before unqualified exotic intervention.

Possibility of revolution is not proportionality.

## 84. Article 9 — Environmental and lifecycle integrity

Lifecycle:

material extraction -> manufacture -> transport -> assembly -> operation -> maintenance -> upgrade -> shutdown -> decommissioning -> reuse, storage, or disposal

The ledger includes energy, cooling, water, cryogens, rare materials, hazardous components, service life, repairability, waste, decommissioning cost, and future custodian.

A project cannot call itself clean by moving its burden elsewhere.

## 85. Article 10 — Dual-use governance

Potential pathways include sensing, surveillance, communication, shielding penetration, navigation, strategic infrastructure, material analysis, energy systems, and military use.

Dual-use classes:

DU0 = no credible pathway

DU1 = general scientific capability with common dual use

DU2 = plausible specialized surveillance or strategic use

DU3 = high-consequence capability requiring controlled review

DU4 = direct coercive, destructive, or destabilizing potential

Possible responses include staged disclosure, secure independent review, omission of operationally dangerous parameters, delayed release, access control, international oversight, or full publication where secrecy would create greater danger.

## 86. Article 11 — Justice and burden distribution

The ledger identifies:

benefit recipients

risk bearers

workers exposed

communities affected

future custodians

data subjects

funding authorities

decision authorities

exit rights

remedy paths

Biological systems must not be used as exploratory detectors or coupling media merely because conventional sensors are inadequate.

## 87. Article 12 — Clean Release

Clean Release = Safe Shutdown + Accounting Closure + Hazard Disposition + Data Preservation + Claim Correction + Responsibility Assignment + Handoff or Decommissioning

Release modes include routine shutdown, temporary suspension, transfer, retirement, emergency termination, public release, commercial release, security-controlled release, and long-term monitored closure.

The project remains in RELEASE HOLD while stored energy, hazardous material, inaccessible data, public overclaim, unassigned custody, unresolved incident, dependent users, or unfunded monitoring remains.

## 88. Stewardship qualification levels

SQ0 = unreviewed

SQ1 = documentation qualified

SQ2 = low-risk research qualified

SQ3 = physical experiment qualified

SQ4 = controlled-coupling qualified

SQ5 = contained prototype qualified

SQ6 = application qualified

SQ7 = scale and release qualified

Current dark-sector candidates do not qualify for SQ4 or above.

The Safe Analog Laboratory may qualify methodology at SQ3 within its declared low-risk envelope.

## 90. Risk bands

R0 = negligible and routine

R1 = low and readily reversible

R2 = moderate, bounded, manageable

R3 = high consequence or difficult recovery

R4 = catastrophic, propagating, or inadequately understood

Current DSSP work should remain in R0-R1 unless a separately qualified facility authorizes higher risk.

## 91. Independent stop authority

Every physical experiment above R1 requires a Safety Officer who can stop operation without approval from the experimental lead, funder, commercial partner, security official, or analysis team.

The stop path must be technically capable, physically accessible, logged, protected, and tested.

## 92. Scale coordinates

Scale includes:

power

duration

range

replication count

distribution

autonomy

connectivity

user population

dependency

authority concentration

A low-power device deployed everywhere may be more consequential than a large isolated apparatus.

Staged scaling:

single apparatus -> replicated laboratory system -> multi-site research network -> restricted pilot -> monitored deployment -> broader deployment

Every stage requires new evidence, incident review, updated load and dual-use ledgers, monitoring, rollback, and release plans.

## 93. Commercial and security boundaries

Commercialization requires independent review of evidence and safety, auditability despite intellectual-property limits, explicit maintenance and end-of-life duties, and marketing language that does not exceed the qualified mechanism.

No analog device may be marketed as dark-sector technology merely because it originated in DSSP.

Security restrictions must identify exact protected information, harm pathway, duration, review authority, independent scientific-access mechanism, and declassification conditions.

Unknown future implications do not justify classifying everything.

## 94. Stewardship paradox

A stewardship system can become harmful if it is opaque, authoritarian, impossible to appeal, captured by incumbents, used to suppress unconventional work, or so burdensome that only powerful institutions can participate.

Therefore DSSP stewardship must remain explicit, proportionate, appealable, versioned, and capable of correcting itself.

The gate protects inquiry. It must not become a throne above inquiry.
