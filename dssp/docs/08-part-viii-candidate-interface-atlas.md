# Part VIII — Candidate Interface Atlas

## 43. Atlas law

Every candidate card must answer:

- What is proposed?
- What is directly measured?
- What is inferred?
- What ordinary process could imitate it?
- What variable does the apparatus control?
- What remains uncontrolled?
- What would falsify the candidate or interface?
- What claim level has actually been reached?
- What applications remain prohibited?

## 44. Candidate maturity coordinates

Evidence maturity C:

C0 = speculative concept

C1 = mathematically formulated candidate

C2 = compatible with part of current evidence

C3 = active experimental search program

C4 = reproducible unexplained candidate signature

C5 = independently confirmed entity or mechanism

C6 = integrated physical fact

Interface maturity I:

I0 = gravitational or cosmological inference only

I1 = passive observable channel identified

I2 = candidate-specific detector coupling tested

I3 = replicated candidate response

I4 = controlled and causally modulated coupling

I5 = reversible transduction

I6 = contained application

I7 = stewardship-qualified scaling

Current dark-sector candidates do not possess a confirmed I4 or higher interface.

Controlling a cavity, beam, magnet, target, telescope, clock, or analysis strategy controls the ordinary apparatus. It does not automatically control the proposed dark-sector source.

## 45. Interface families

IF-GRAV = gravitational and geometric

IF-NUC = nuclear recoil

IF-ELEC = electron, photon, atomic, or material excitation

IF-RES = resonant field

IF-COLL = collider or fixed-target production

IF-IND = indirect annihilation or decay

IF-COS = cosmological expansion and structure

IF-GW = gravitational-wave and compact-object

## 46. Major candidate families

### 46.1 WIMP-like massive particles

Search channels:

- nuclear recoil;
- electron recoil;
- collider missing momentum;
- annihilation or decay products;
- modulation and directional signatures.

Principal backgrounds:

- neutrons;
- radioactive contamination;
- neutrinos;
- surface events;
- charge loss;
- threshold effects.

Current engineering meaning:

Candidate-sensitive detectors exist. Captured, stored, reflected, concentrated, directed, or harvested WIMP populations have not been demonstrated.

### 46.2 Light and sub-GeV dark matter

Search channels:

- ionization;
- low-gap materials;
- phonons;
- photons;
- quasiparticles;
- collective excitations.

Principal danger:

Detector and material behavior near threshold can imitate the target.

Immediate engineering value lies in better low-energy sensors and material metrology, not proven dark-sector technology.

### 46.3 QCD axions and axion-like particles

Search channels:

- resonant conversion in magnetic fields;
- helioscopes;
- light-shining-through-wall systems;
- astrophysical constraints;
- collider or rare-decay production.

Expected resonant signature:

A narrow excess whose frequency, linewidth, magnetic-field scaling, cavity response, rescan behavior, and independent recurrence match the candidate model.

Apparatus control:

Magnetic field, cavity tuning, geometry, quality factor, receiver, and scan strategy.

Not controlled:

The presumed galactic field.

### 46.4 Ultralight scalar and vector fields

Possible observables:

- atomic-clock shifts;
- interferometer length change;
- force on test masses;
- spin precession;
- resonant mechanical or electromagnetic response;
- coherent distributed-sensor signatures.

Principal counterfeits:

Clock drift, seismic noise, magnetic fields, thermal expansion, laser noise, shared timing, and environmental periodicity.

### 46.5 Dark photons and vector portals

Possible channels:

- missing energy or momentum;
- displaced decays;
- dilepton resonances;
- monophoton signatures;
- long-lived downstream particles;
- resonant electromagnetic response;
- coherent force signals.

A particle produced in a collider would not automatically be the cosmological dark matter. Mass, coupling, lifetime, abundance, and astrophysical behavior must reconcile across interfaces.

### 46.6 Scalar and Higgs portals

Possible channels:

- invisible or exotic Higgs decay;
- long-lived particles;
- rare decays;
- fifth-force behavior;
- oscillating fundamental constants;
- cosmological phase-transition signatures.

Claims of vacuum manipulation remain quarantined unless a specific field, coupling, causal response, and closed ledger are demonstrated.

### 46.7 Neutrino portal and sterile or heavy neutral candidates

Possible channels:

- oscillation;
- beta-decay spectra;
- meson decay;
- beam-dump experiments;
- displaced decay;
- cosmology;
- astrophysical lines.

Neutrino-model complexity makes ordinary and hidden explanations especially difficult to separate.

### 46.8 Composite dark matter and dark QCD

Possible collider signatures:

- emerging jets;
- semivisible jets;
- dark showers;
- displaced decays;
- soft unclustered energy;
- invisible shower components.

The observed event may be several transformations removed from the proposed hidden interaction. Simulation dependence and classifier domain shift are first-class uncertainties.

### 46.9 Self-interacting dark matter

Primary interface:

Astrophysical structure and gravitational inference.

Possible consequences:

Halo cores, cluster behavior, substructure, halo shape, and gravothermal evolution.

Baryonic feedback is a major competing explanation.

No local actuator is established.

### 46.10 Primordial black holes

Possible channels:

Microlensing, gravitational waves, accretion, dynamical effects, Hawking radiation at low mass, and subsolar compact-object searches.

Black hole detected != primordial black hole

Primordial black hole detected != all dark matter

Their hypothetical proximity would represent an extreme hazard, not a plausible harvesting pathway.

### 46.11 Indirect annihilation or decay

Search channels:

Gamma rays, neutrinos, antimatter, electrons, positrons, cosmic backgrounds, and target environments such as dwarf galaxies or the Galactic center.

Principal backgrounds:

Pulsars, cosmic-ray interactions, unresolved sources, gas maps, astrophysical neutrinos, propagation uncertainty, and target-density uncertainty.

### 46.12 Modified gravity

Possible discriminants:

Galaxy dynamics, lensing, clusters, large-scale structure, binary systems, gravitational-wave propagation, and equivalence-principle tests.

DSSP does not assume dark matter and modified gravity must be mutually exclusive.

### 46.13 Cosmological constant

Primary interface:

Cosmic expansion, distances, structure, and lensing.

No local extraction or modulation interface is known.

Vacuum energy in cosmological equations does not imply accessible laboratory fuel.

### 46.14 Dynamical dark energy

Possible forms:

Quintessence-like fields, k-essence, interacting dark energy, modified gravity, evolving vacuum behavior, or a misfit among datasets and parameterizations.

A flexible equation-of-state fit does not identify a local field.

### 46.15 Interacting dark energy and dark matter

A cosmological transfer term may alter expansion and structure growth, but no local exchange interface has been established.

## 47. Cross-interface confirmation

Strong candidate confirmation requires one parameter structure to reconcile multiple channels.

Direct recoil + collider mediator + indirect signal + cosmological abundance

Superficial similarity is insufficient.

## 48. Weak-coupling paradox

Many viable candidates are attractive because they interact weakly enough to survive cosmological time and evade detection.

Weak coupling -> invisibility and longevity

Weak coupling -> very low interaction rate

Very low interaction rate -> poor energy-transfer capacity

Any engineering proposal must explain selective strong coupling inside the device, weak coupling outside, reversibility, containment, and closed accounting.

Without that bridge, harvesting language contradicts the candidate’s own premise.

## 49. Likely early engineering benefits

The most plausible early products of dark-sector research are:

- lower-background detectors;
- quantum-limited amplifiers;
- improved resonators;
- better cryogenics;
- stable clocks;
- distributed sensor networks;
- precision gravitational mapping;
- improved covariance methods;
- anomaly-classification systems;
- multimessenger data fusion;
- stronger provenance and review infrastructure.

These can succeed even if the preferred candidate fails.
